# Source and License Policy

No biblical corpus, morphology, lexicon, commentary, organizational document, model, or derived dataset is approved merely because it is accessible online. Approval requires the exact artifact, revision, SHA-256 digest, license text, attribution, intended uses, and a named legal/repository decision in `configs/data/source_registry.json`.

Approved, immutable source decisions recorded on 2026-08-11:

- eBible KJV 1769/2006 edition: public domain outside the United Kingdom, with eBible/Crosswire attribution preserved and the stated UK printing restriction retained.
- Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible 2.2: WLC text is public domain; lemma and morphology work is CC BY 4.0. Unicode normalization is prohibited by the import contract.
- Faithlife SBLGNT 1.2: CC BY 4.0 with Society of Biblical Literature and Logos Bible Software attribution.
- MorphGNT SBLGNT 6.12: morphology and lemmatization are CC BY-SA 3.0, while embedded SBLGNT text follows its controlling license. Public model-weight distribution remains blocked pending downstream share-alike review.
- Open Scriptures HebrewLexicon: repository files are CC BY 4.0; its Brown-Driver-Briggs and Strong Hebrew/Aramaic dictionary sources are identified as public domain.
- Abbott-Smith Manual Greek Lexicon of the New Testament: the marked-up source is identified by its source project as public domain.

The exact revisions, upstream downloads, license evidence files, SHA-256 values, canonical artifact digests, attributions, use scopes, and owner approval decisions are in `configs/data/source_registry.json`, `source_packages.json`, and `lexicon_packages.json`. This is repository governance, not legal advice. Textus Receptus, Septuagint, and Vulgate candidates remain unapproved.

Lexicon definitions are auxiliary linguistic evidence. They are stored and searched separately and are never exported into the exact-Scripture quotation corpus.

Proprietary translations, lexicons, commentaries, sermons, study notes, and manuscript databases are excluded by default. Fair-use assumptions are not a substitute for permission in a distributable training corpus.
