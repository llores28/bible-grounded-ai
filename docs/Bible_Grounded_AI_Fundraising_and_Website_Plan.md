## 1. Public project definition

## 3. Legal and financial setup before accepting money

Choose one structure before launch:

### Option A — Personal/project crowdfunding

Fastest for the proof of concept. State clearly that contributions are **not represented as tax-deductible charitable donations**. Keep a separate project checking account and complete records of every receipt and expense. Crowdfunding proceeds can be taxable depending on the facts, whether contributors receive anything, and how the campaign is structured. Obtain advice from a Texas CPA or attorney before launch.

### Option B — Fiscal sponsorship

Recommended if the work will be genuinely charitable or educational and tax-deductible contributions are important. A qualified 501(c)(3) fiscal sponsor receives and controls charitable funds, handles acknowledgements and oversight, and typically charges an administrative fee. The project must fit the sponsor's charitable mission, and the sponsor—not the project founder—retains legal control over donated funds.

### Option C — Form a nonprofit

Potentially appropriate later, but it adds formation, governance, reporting and compliance work. Do not delay the research pilot solely to form a nonprofit unless advisers conclude it is necessary.

### Required controls

- Separate project finances from household and consulting income.
- Record contributor, date, gross amount, fee, net amount and any promised benefit.
- Publish a monthly budget-versus-actual summary without exposing donor private information.
- Use written approval for purchases above a chosen threshold, such as $1,000.
- Never call a payment tax deductible unless a qualified tax-exempt organization is actually receiving it.
- Avoid investment language, financial-return promises, equity, revenue sharing or cryptocurrency unless securities counsel approves the structure.
- Create a refund and campaign-change policy before accepting money.

## 6. Ready-to-use page content

### Home page

**Hero title:**  
**Building a Scripture-Grounded AI for Biblical Study and Everyday Moral Reasoning**

**Hero paragraph:**  
We are developing an open and carefully evaluated research system that can study Scripture in Biblical Hebrew, Aramaic, Koine Greek and Latin, then apply clearly identified biblical principles to questions involving family, finance, work, relationships, technology and spiritual life. The system will cite evidence, distinguish Scripture from interpretation, and admit uncertainty.

**Primary button:** Support the Proof of Concept  
**Secondary button:** Read the Development Plan

**Trust statement:**  
This project will not claim that AI is conscious, divinely inspired, or able to replace Scripture, prayer, pastoral care, professional advice or human judgment.

**Current milestone block:**  
Our first goal is a reproducible Apertus 1.5B proof of concept developed on an RTX 5090 workstation, supported by retrieval-backed citations and independent language and theological review.

### Why This Matters

Many AI systems can produce convincing religious language without reliably distinguishing an exact biblical text, a translation choice, a theological interpretation and practical advice. This project is designed around traceability. Users should be able to see which passage, manuscript transcription, linguistic analysis or moral principle supports an answer—and where reasonable Christians disagree.

### How It Will Work

Present six compact stages:

1. Curate legally usable biblical-language and scholarly data.
2. Establish the untouched model's baseline performance.
3. Train biblical-language capabilities using memory-efficient methods.
4. Add manuscript and Scripture retrieval with verified citations.
5. Train against a reviewed Biblical Moral Constitution and realistic life cases.
6. Test for hallucinations, bias, unsafe guidance and theological overstatement.

Add a visible distinction:

- **The model learns linguistic and reasoning patterns.**
- **Retrieval supplies exact texts, readings and citations.**
- **Qualified reviewers evaluate claims and failures.**

### Roadmap and Budget

Display the $15,000 table and four public milestones from this plan. Add a progress bar only if it can be updated accurately. List gross contributions, platform fees and net funds separately.

### Research and Safeguards

Publish these commitments:

- Use only public-domain, open-license or properly licensed training material.
- Preserve dataset-level provenance and license records.
- Separate explicit biblical teaching, strong biblical inference and tradition-specific interpretation.
- Label Seventh-day Adventist interpretations transparently where relevant.
- Never fabricate Hebrew, Aramaic, Greek or Latin text.
- Require retrieval verification for manuscript and verse claims.
- Treat medical, legal, financial, abuse and self-harm matters with appropriate professional and safety boundaries.
- Publish known limitations and negative test results—not only demonstrations that look successful.

### Progress and Reports

Create a repeatable update template:

**Date — Milestone — Funds used — Work completed — Measurements — Failures/risks — Next action**

Post at least one update per month during active fundraising and development.

### Team and Advisers

Include LAN's relevant experience in systems administration, Python, automation, Docker, AI workflows and project implementation. Do not present LAN as a biblical-language scholar. Add scholars, pastors, attorneys, accountants or advisers only after they explicitly consent to be named. Identify paid, volunteer and advisory roles.

### FAQ

Include:

**Is this AI supposed to speak for God?**  
No. It is a human-designed research system that applies a documented biblical framework and remains capable of error.

**Why not use an existing chatbot?**  
The project emphasizes source provenance, biblical-language testing, manuscript-aware retrieval, transparent interpretation labels and public evaluations.

**Will the model replace pastors or scholars?**  
No. It is intended to support study and structured reasoning, not replace spiritual leadership or qualified expertise.

**Is the project only for Seventh-day Adventists?**  
The core system will distinguish broad biblical evidence from denominational interpretation. SDA perspectives will be labeled rather than presented as if no other Christian interpretation exists.

**Are contributions tax deductible?**  
Use only the answer that matches the chosen legal structure. For a personal campaign: “No representation is made that contributions are tax deductible. Consult your tax adviser.” For a fiscal sponsor: use the sponsor's approved language.

**What happens if the full goal is not reached?**  
Funds will be applied to the earliest unfunded milestone in the published order. Material scope changes will be disclosed on the project site and campaign page.

## 11. Success measurements

### Fundraising

- 20%–30% funded before public launch.
- At least 100 individual conversations or targeted messages.
- At least 20 sponsor or partnership requests.
- Average contribution and donor conversion rate tracked without public donor profiling.
- Monthly budget-versus-actual report published.

### Website

- A new visitor can explain the mission, first milestone, budget and limitations after one visit.
- At least 5% of qualified campaign-page visitors take a support, contact or subscribe action; establish the actual baseline before setting later targets.
- Mobile pages have no clipped tables, unreadable text or broken embeds.
- Every progress claim links to a report, evaluation or artifact.

### Project accountability

- Dataset license register exists before training.
- Unmodified-model baseline is published before adaptation results.
- Negative findings and failed experiments are reported.
- No “biblical scholar,” “safe,” “moral,” or “manuscript-aware” capability claim is made without defined tests and results.
